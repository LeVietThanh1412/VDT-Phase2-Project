# Two-target physical PIE acceptance

The saved MainMapLevel enemy uses actor-backed **Patrol** mode, starting at `TP_ShipEnemy_AStarDestination` (A), then `TP_ShipEnemy_OpenSeaDestination` (B), repeatedly. Both references are saved on the placed EnemyNavigation component. No setup or coordinate editing is needed before Play. Historical single-destination reports describe previous configurations.

1. Press Play from the saved level. Verify both references resolve, A is selected, a fresh native A* route is generated, and actual physics velocity/position change. Check that AIThrottle/AIRudder match SetDriveCommand outputs.
2. Collect physical hull positions and generated routes at 0.5 simulation-second intervals. Independently compare the track and every route against the supplied OSM land polygons and the 125 m exclusion. Grid cells are 100 m; accepted cells are wholly outside the exclusion. Missing terrain collision is unknown water depth.
3. Continue to A. Arrival requires a clear final segment to the live actor, distance <=60 m, horizontal speed <0.5 m/s and 2 seconds settled. Record the measured distance and arrival event. Debug colors: blue A, orange B, red requested destination, green grid goal, cyan route, yellow active waypoint, magenta exact endpoint.
4. Verify arrival switches once to B and generates a new route from the current physical position. Let the ship turn using its existing rudder. Continue to B and then back to A. Record all three arrival events; a route calculation or simulated fixture arrival is not physical acceptance.
5. Check for failed plans, indefinite recovery, repeated arrival without travel, unsafe grid cells, and land/buffer crossings throughout the run. Preserve failed runs when changing code.
6. Stop PIE, record both full saved target transforms, temporarily move A to independently verified accepted open water, and save. Start a new PIE session; verify its exact new endpoint, fresh route and physical progress. Stop PIE, restore A's complete original transform, and save. B must stay unchanged.
7. Start a new PIE session from the restored saved configuration. Confirm A-first startup, both saved references, fresh routing and physical commands. Compile and validate BP_ShipEnemy; verify protected player/Water/Cesium assets retain their hashes.
8. In a separate disposable PIE session, exercise the Cesium georeference setter and Unreal world-origin rebasing. Sample frames around both transitions. Confirm stable geographic targets/routes, exact target endpoint alignment, no geographic jump, and neutral transition commands. Do not save PIE coordinate changes.

`Tools/SeaNavigation/verify_two_target_pie.py` records the bounded real loop test. `validate_two_target_telemetry.py` independently checks geometry and command delivery. `relocate_two_target_test.py` saves/restores the relocation fixture; `verify_two_target_origin.py` tests disposable origin changes. Native ShipEnemyNavigation automation covers isolated logic and is reported separately from physical runs.

The supplied polygons encode coastlines, not bathymetry. Agreement between every Cesium shoreline and OSM remains outside the sampled alignment checks. A target placed in blocked water or outside coverage must fail visibly with neutral commands.
